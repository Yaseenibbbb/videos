# يومٌ عاديّ بقي في القلب — One-line Drawing

Style test 1 of 3 for the Hikaytomi Reels series «أمي، احكي لي». The same on-screen script and timing are used in the crayon-book and urban-sketch versions, so the only variable is the style.

## Brief (as agreed)

- **Source:** Q235. The printed question reads «ما اللحظة التي لو عادت، لتمنّيتِ أن تعيشيها بنفس الشعور مرة أخرى؟». On screen it's adapted for speech: «ما اللحظة التي تتمنّين أن تعيشيها مرّةً أخرى؟».
- **Form:** emotional reflection over hypothetical imagery. The family scene is a **fictional vignette**, not a real memory, and the film never claims to know what any mother would answer.
- **No voice.** The words are written on screen, with sound effects and a quiet solo-instrument score. All text is Modern Standard Arabic.
- **Format:** 1080×1920 at 24 fps, about 56 s. Text stays inside the central safe area: y 260–1500 on a 1920-high frame, clear of Instagram's top bar and caption/button zone.
- **Ending:** a soft call to action: the real «حكاية أمي» book mockup, «أسئلة لحفظ الذكريات والحكايات», hikaytomi.com. No price, no urgency.
- **Facts:** the film makes no factual claims, so there's nothing to source.

## Three candidate structures

1. **One day out of many (chosen).** The line opens at speed, ruling a month of calendar days in one serpentine stroke. It circles one ordinary square, and the camera dives into it. Inside, the same line draws the lunch: children playing, Mum calling, one late. The line becomes time as growth marks climb a door frame, then morphs the table into the present, where grown child and mother sit facing each other. The final stroke draws the book outline for the call to action. *Why:* "one day of her life" is literally one square of many, and "one continuous line" is literally a life. The dive into one square is the signature shot.
2. **Line as graph.** A heartbeat-like line of the years, with each peak a scene. Rejected: it implies drama and highs, but the point is that the chosen day was ordinary.
3. **Two lines meeting.** The mother's line and the child's line start in opposite corners and meet at the table. Rejected: it fights the "one line" invariant, and the meeting reads as a reunion, which pulls toward loss.

## Logline and arc

One ink line rules a whole month, chooses one unremarkable day, and inside it finds a lunch worth reliving. Then it keeps running, through the years, to the chair across from her.

- **Setup:** many days, one chosen (0–10 s).
- **Development:** the ordinary lunch: play, the call, the latecomer (10–21 s).
- **Turn:** the line becomes time: growth marks, the house morphs (21–26 s). The laugh around the table is the warm peak and the **one accent**, the brand rose `#bb6a71`, on the laugh's curl (26–31 s).
- **Stop:** the nib halts, a teardrop blot, silence (31.4–33 s).
- **Ending:** in the present, two chairs and two cups. Her speech-line curls into a tiny drawing of the old table: she tells, we listen (33–49 s). The line draws the book's outline and the real cover develops inside it (49.8–55.8 s).

## Benchmark

- **Learn from** Cavandoli's *La Linea*: the line is world and character, and its transformations are the transitions. Also Picasso's light drawings (Mili, 1949): one confident gesture reads as a figure.
- **Don't take:** La Linea's character, gags, voice or profile. No specific illustration is copied.

## Type

Text is written on like the line, right to left (Arabic reading direction), in **Aref Ruqaa**, a ruqʿa hand: the everyday Arabic handwriting and the closest Arabic equivalent of the style's handwriting face. Ink is at about 84 %, with no box, centred low at y≈1430 behind a soft paper-coloured halo. Each line is written on in about 0.25 + 0.018 s per character, then fades out in 0.45 s. The call-to-action brand text uses **El Messiri** (the store's own typeface), in the brand plum `#6f363c`.
