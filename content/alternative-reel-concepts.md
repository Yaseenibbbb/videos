# Alternative Reel concepts for Hikaytomi
_Date: 27 October 2026. Expansion following the request for concepts beyond «قبل ما تصير أمي»._

These are distinct storytelling formats. Each can draw from the journal while giving viewers its own reason to watch and return. Growth mechanisms below are creative hypotheses to test.

The source journal was read for the initial plan. Printed page references use its numbering, with PDF viewer page = printed page + 3. All situations below are proposed fiction, not customer stories or biographical claims.

## 1. كل حكاية لها وجهين — One moment, two perspectives

**Premise:** replay one ordinary family moment from two people's viewpoints. The second version reveals a missing piece and changes the meaning of the first.

This can cover humour, misunderstandings, affection, boundaries and different preferences. Sometimes the mother understands something differently; sometimes the child does. Neither viewpoint has to be declared wrong.

**Viewer promise:** “You saw what happened. Now discover what the other person saw.”

### Pilot: الشخص الناقص من الصورة

- **Opening:** a family is finally ready to eat. Mum asks for a photo; everyone groans and starts rearranging.
- **First perspective:** the adult child sees a small family meal turning into a photo session again.
- **Second perspective:** Mum scrolls through the previous gatherings. Everyone is in the pictures except the person who took them.
- **Turn:** the child notices, sets a timer and puts a chair in the frame for her.
- **Payoff:** Mum rushes into the photo, someone laughs, and the photograph catches the imperfect, happy moment.
- **Closing question:** «مين كان دايم خلف الكاميرا في بيتكم؟»
- **Book connection:** favourite memories with children, printed p.59; a moment to live again, p.80.
- **Style:** crayon-book. Replay matching compositions with page turns; use the same action and a different camera emphasis.

### Other episodes

- Two siblings think the other was the favourite; the family conversation reveals different needs and different memories, without declaring a universal explanation.
- A relative always leaves gatherings early. A second viewpoint reveals a quiet ritual waiting at home that they look forward to.
- An adult child keeps sending photos of ordinary meals. Mum thinks they need cooking advice; they are really trying to recreate a taste they miss.

**Reason to follow:** the recurring perspective reversal. The subject can stay in the present and expand beyond motherhood without losing the family theme.

## 2. لو الأشياء تحكي — If objects could speak

**Premise:** an ordinary household object narrates a small family story. The object has a specific personality and only knows what it has witnessed.

**Viewer promise:** “That thing you barely notice has a story.”

### Pilot: الكرسي اللي عند الباب

- **Opening, chair speaking:** «أنا أقدم كرسي في البيت… وكل مرة يشترون جديد، يخلّوني.»
- Someone sits to tie shoes before a first day at work.
- Another person stands on the chair to hang decorations.
- A guest pulls it into a crowded gathering.
- Today, someone suggests replacing it. Another person sits down and starts telling the story of how it arrived.
- **Payoff:** the chair is repaired and pulled into the gathering again. Its role continues.
- **Question:** «أي غرض في بيتكم له حكاية ما يعرفها الضيوف؟»
- **Book connection:** childhood home, p.7; family story to keep and retell, p.87.
- **Style:** one-line. The chair's outline leads continuously into each witnessed scene; a repair can be the one meaningful accent.

### Other episodes

- **The tea tray:** convinced it is the most important family member because every visitor meets it first. A playful account of hospitality.
- **The suitcase:** proud of travelling, then discovers its favourite trip was carrying gifts between relatives.
- **The recipe notebook:** annoyed that everyone claims the recipe is theirs; its margins reveal several people's contributions.
- **The spare key:** tells a story of neighbours helping each other.

Give the object character through voice and timing. Faces are optional; a talking object need not become a cartoon mascot.

**Reason to follow:** curiosity about the next object, plus viewers recognising objects in their own homes. This can become a signature visual collection.

## 3. الخميس في بيتنا — An ongoing animated family series

**Premise:** a fictional family gathers every Thursday. Each episode begins with a tiny domestic situation and uncovers a difference between what its members know about each other.

**Recurring cast:**

- A mother with a dry sense of humour and a current interest outside the home.
- An adult son and an adult daughter with different habits.
- A grandmother who is affectionate, opinionated and occasionally contradicts everybody.
- A neighbour who appears when the story needs them.

Characters need consistent motives and visual identities. Create a small character bible before production. The audience can have favourites and recognise recurring jokes.

### Pilot: مين صاحب الوصفة؟

- Everyone claims the dish on the table comes from their side of the family.
- Three characters confidently demonstrate three different “essential” ingredients.
- Grandma tastes it and says: «الوصفة أصلاً علّمتني إياها جارتنا.»
- They pause, then ask about the neighbour. Grandma starts describing how they used to exchange plates.
- **Payoff:** the next Thursday they leave an extra plate for their current neighbour.
- **Book connection:** learning to cook, p.40; inherited recipe, p.41; neighbours, p.37.
- **Style:** crayon-book, with warm ensemble comedy and simple readable poses.

### A possible six-episode run

1. Who owns the recipe?
2. Everyone guesses Mum's favourite breakfast; each guesses their own.
3. Grandma recognises a phrase the adult son thinks he invented.
4. Mum wants one afternoon for a class; the family must organise the gathering themselves, with comic results.
5. An old friend's visit exposes a hilarious difference between two people's recollection of one event.
6. Everyone brings one ordinary thing for a family keepsake box; Grandma brings something newly made.

Resolve each episode. Carry relationships, preferences and a small recurring joke forward. Do not make every ending a lesson or require a viewer to find part two.

**Reason to follow:** attachment to characters and anticipation of the next gathering. It requires more continuity work than stand-alone films, but it gives the account an actual series identity.

## 4. مكتب الكلام اللي ما انقال — The office of unsent words

**Premise:** an imaginary post office receives sentences people never managed to say. Each letter opens into the ordinary scene behind it.

These can be apologies, thanks, awkward compliments, invitations, questions or affectionate jokes. Keep a varied tone.

### Pilot: «طلعتي صح… بس لا تتعودين»

- A letter arrives containing an embarrassed admission.
- Flashback: a mother teaches her adult child to cook. The child insists they can improvise.
- An overfilled pot boils over. They silently send Mum a picture.
- Instead of a lecture, she sends one practical fix.
- Return to the letter: «طلعتي صح… بس لا تتعودين.»
- **Payoff:** Mum writes back: «متعودة من زمان.»
- **Question:** «إيش جملة ودك تقولها لأمك بطريقتك؟»
- **Book connection:** kitchen anecdote, p.42; words that remained unsaid, p.81.
- **Style:** one-line, with the envelope fold extending into the scene and returning to the reply.

### Other episodes

- Thanking a sibling for taking over an ordinary responsibility.
- Asking an old friend to come for tea without making the invitation feel grand.
- Telling Mum, “I would like your company,” after years of phoning mostly to ask for help.

**Reason to follow:** the fictional office is a repeatable world; viewers anticipate what arrives next. It also offers an easy, private action: write or send the sentence.

Keep early episodes light and specific. The concept does not need tragedy to work.

## 5. باب الاحتمالات — A door into a possible day

**Premise:** a question opens an illustrated doorway into an imagined day. Show a person's current wish as a small visual adventure.

**Viewer promise:** “What would a day look like if this wish had room?”

### Pilot: يوم إجازة من كلمة «لازم»

- **Opening:** someone asks Mum where she would spend a day with no responsibilities.
- A kettle-shaped door opens into a small seaside café.
- She tries drawing a boat; the drawing is crooked. She laughs and starts again.
- The café closes. She is still happily adding details.
- Return home. She puts a sketchbook beside tomorrow's keys.
- **Payoff:** a small real step carries something of the imagined day into the present.
- **Question:** «لو لأمك يوم على كيفها، كيف تقضيه؟»
- **Book connection:** a place to live without responsibilities, p.15; a part of herself still needing attention, p.76; a place she wishes to visit, p.78.
- **Style:** urban-sketch. Ink creates the imagined place, then colour arrives as she participates.

### Other episodes

- A grandmother imagines being a beginner at something and chooses a local class.
- Two siblings imagine a gathering where no one has to host, then arrange a picnic.
- Someone describes a favourite quiet morning; the illustration explores how specific ordinary happiness can be.

Avoid assuming motherhood means someone lost every dream, or that a relative must rescue them. Let the person choose and act.

**Reason to follow:** visual escape, hopeful endings and a new imaginative location in each episode.

## Recommended starting directions

For the user's follower-building goal, the strongest three different bets are:

1. **كل حكاية لها وجهين** — a clear narrative reversal in every film; good for an initial stand-alone pilot.
2. **لو الأشياء تحكي** — a recognisable signature format with an open-ended supply of episode subjects.
3. **الخميس في بيتنا** — the strongest option for an ongoing storyline and recurring characters.

The third is the closest match to wanting a series people follow as entertainment. Start with the recipe pilot, keep the book out of the episode, and place its source question in the caption. If the characters and humour work, build a small run before introducing the journal as something the family uses in a companion film.

These are alternative creative directions, not five simultaneous production commitments. No videos have been rendered or published from these briefs.

## How the product fits these concepts

The account and profile identify Hikaytomi. Most episodes can end on their own payoff; questions can move to the caption when an on-screen question would weaken the joke or ending.

Occasional companion films can show how an episode's question becomes a conversation and how that conversation can be kept in the journal. A direct product film can explain the journal separately.

For fictional films use a clear caption such as: «حكاية متخيّلة، مستوحاة من سؤال في حكاية أمي». For future audience-submitted stories, obtain permission before adapting or identifying their family.

## Production handoff

Use the shared Lemo-Opuscar, vertical-format, Arabic text and audio checks in `instagram-reels-strategy.md`. Replace its first-film brief with the selected concept and pilot above.

- Match the animation method to the premise: replay through crayon page turns, object-linked continuous drawing, or an imagined place arriving through ink and wash.
- Preserve joined, correctly shaped Arabic and full-phrase reveals.
- Make the visual action carry the joke or discovery.
- Leave room for reactions, physical humour and a satisfying ending.
- Do not copy the upstream demo's plot or present a proposed fiction as a real testimonial.
